## Concrete residual corrections that preserve the teaching contract

1. **`ask-me-questions` metadata contradicts its exceptions.** The description promises never to declare facts, but the body permits citation and safety statements. Make the description an accurate questions-led purpose and trigger statement, or explicitly qualify it by the existing exceptions. This changes neither exception.
2. **`linked-notes` still equates paraphrasing with transfer.** Its last principle says own-word paraphrase is itself a transfer demonstration, despite the research warning against that inference. Describe the exit as a learner-authored, source-checked note. Do not silently add an examination.
3. **`ask-me-questions` still equates constructing insight with transfer.** The actual exit is the learner choosing to finish. State that rule accurately without claiming a new-scenario test took place.
4. **`learn-by-doing`'s final summary assumes E5–E7.** The protocol permits selecting 3–7 exercises and requires E4 in code mode; it does not require all late ladder stages. Say that selected E5–E7 tasks target transfer and that generation itself does not verify completion. Preserve the selection range.
5. **`flashcards` calls cards a transfer-test exit.** Cards are future practice material. Preserve the artifact-generation completion rule and distinguish it from later retrieval evaluation.

These findings are direct comparisons of current `skills/*/SKILL.md` files with their own detailed protocols and the existing research. They are not claims that any upstream author discovered these exact local bugs.

## Rigid prohibitions: what to retain and what not to infer

Some prohibitions express intentional product behavior: learner-written note bodies, learner-first explanations, source-closed retrieval, no numeric assessment score. Removing them to imitate another repository would change functionality. Their exceptions must remain visible and consistent.

Other absolutes are empirical overclaims: calling every artifact a transfer test, implying a fixed count is optimal, or asserting universal performance. Replace those with exact observable outcomes. Anthropic recommends matching instruction freedom to task fragility; this supports strict output formats where required and judgment where variation is legitimate, not blanket rigidity. [Authoring guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices)

Superpowers' pressure-testing reference reports 100% compliance in its own example, dated 2025-10-03. That is a bounded maintainer report, not evidence of perfect generalization across providers, versions, learners, or our skills. Its useful contribution is adversarial baseline comparison and iterative retesting. [Primary test reference](https://github.com/obra/superpowers/blob/5bf4e78011075bcfc0dc295f0724994cd123ee71/skills/writing-skills/testing-skills-with-subagents.md)

## What was actually verified

Implementation follow-up: all five residual wording corrections listed above were applied after this audit snapshot. They qualify unsupported evidence claims and align metadata with existing exceptions; they add no new transfer-exam requirement. This does not close the runtime-evaluation gaps in the matrix.

The prior [validation report](../skill-quality-validation.md) records static validation, resource closure, mutation checks, and simulated instruction traces. This audit inspected those claims and current files; it did not independently rerun the earlier commands or recreate their transcripts. No cross-provider runtime, latency, cost, retention, or learning-outcome result was established here.

The next measurable step is a fixed suite with before/after and no-skill baselines, repeated trials, positive and negative discovery prompts, and multi-turn artifact checks. Grade source fidelity, waiting boundaries, exceptions, user authorship, output paths, and actual completion separately. Such results can establish reliability on tested conditions, never “all best practices applied” or 100% future performance. Anthropic explicitly distinguishes trials, transcripts, outcomes, and harnesses; those distinctions prevent a plausible answer from being mistaken for a successful execution. [Evaluation guidance](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)
